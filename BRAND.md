# Art Basil brand

## In one line

Art Basil is a free, community-built guide to art near you.

**Tagline:** Find art near you

## Who it's for

- **Art-goers** looking for something to do this week.
- **Artists and people who love looking at art**, who want to know what's on beyond the places they already know.
- **Visitors and tourists** who want to find what's good in a city they don't know.

Venues and artists are part of the picture too, as the people who make the listings worth reading and who can help keep them right.

## The name

Art Basil is a play on Art Basel, the luxury art fair, and it pokes gently at the high fine art world: exclusive, hushed, expensive, and invite-only. Art Basil is the opposite: free, loud, local, and for anyone. Basil is a herb you can grow on a windowsill, which is a good counterweight to a fair for collectors.

It's a joke told with affection, not an attack, and we are not affiliated with Art Basel. Our look should never imitate theirs (clean, minimal, Swiss-style).

## Values

**Discovery.** Help people stumble onto art they didn't know about. A good visit to Art Basil ends with "I had no idea that was on".

**Community.** Visitors help build it. Anyone can add an event, suggest a venue, or tell us what's wrong. Credit people when they help.

**Creativity.** The site should feel made by someone who likes art: playful, a bit messy in a good way, never corporate.

**Local.** Every city is its own place, with its own page and colour, and its own small venues alongside the big ones. We'd rather be right about one neighbourhood than vague about a country.

**Openness.** Free, no account, no gatekeeping. Open to any venue, large or small, and any visitor. The code is open too.

## How we sound

- Plain and warm, like a friend who knows what's on. No art-speak.
- Playful, but never at the expense of the information. Dates, times, and places have to be right.
- Say what happened and what to do next, especially when something goes wrong. Never blame the visitor.
- Sentence case. Short sentences. Skip words like "curated" and "immersive" unless a venue uses them.

## How we look

- **Style:** Y2K and Kid Pix. Paint blobs and stickers, with thick black outlines and hard offset shadows.
- **Colour:** one accent colour per region, applied the same way everywhere. The palette and rules for adding new regions are in [ADDING_A_REGION.md](ADDING_A_REGION.md). Black is the accent on pages that don't belong to a region.
- **Type:** Bagel Fat One for titles and region names, Inter for everything else.
- **Motion and effects:** fun, optional, and respectful. Visitors choose their own cursor and trail effect, and everything calms down for `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Logo and favicon:** not decided yet. They should feel hand-made and a little irreverent, read clearly at 16 pixels, and hint at basil and art without copying Art Basel's wordmark.

## What we're not

- Not an auction house, a gallery, or a marketplace. We don't sell art.
- Not exclusive. No guest lists, no "must-see" rankings that decide who matters.
- Not a review site. We say what's on, not what to think about it.
- Not corporate. If a design choice would look at home in a bank, we've gone the wrong way.

## Using this

When a choice comes up (a new feature, a button label, a colour), ask:

1. Does it help someone **discover** something?
2. Does it make it easier for people to **contribute**?
3. Does it feel **creative** and not corporate?
4. Does it respect the **local** place it's about?
5. Is it **open**, with no cost, account, or gate?

If it fails two of these, think again.
